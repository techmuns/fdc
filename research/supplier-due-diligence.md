# FDC Supplier Due-Diligence — plain-language, evidence-backed

**Why this exists:** before we tell FDC to move volume to a supplier or lean on one, we check them the way an equity-research analyst would — capacity, money, risk, quality — and explain **in plain words why we're saying it**, with a real source behind every fact. Where we can't find something reliable, we write "not found," never a guess.

**The four questions we answer for every supplier, then a plain bottom line:**
1. **Can they take your extra volume?** (capacity)
2. **Are they financially healthy enough to rely on?** (money)
3. **Any warning signs?** (risk / news)
4. **Can they do pharma-quality?** (fit)

## What we found across all 20 — the short version

**1. The suppliers you OVERPAY are mostly rock-solid — so push price, don't switch.** Gujarat Ambuja (dextrose) is India's biggest maize processor and effectively debt-free; Sukhjit (dextrose), Chemco (bottles) and TPAC (bottles) are all healthy and well-rated. You're giving away margin to strong companies — negotiate, don't replace them.

**2. Several "move your volume here" suggestions DON'T hold up — this is the big one, and it's exactly the gap the founder spotted.** When we tried to verify the cheaper alternatives:
- **Silk Polypack** and **Suraj Blow Plast** — **no public record anywhere.** We cannot confirm they exist as real factories.
- **PIK Plastics, Premier Plastic, Mars Plastics** — tiny one-owner firms with **no audited accounts, no verifiable capacity, no pharma certificates.**
- **Cincorp** — isn't even registered as a packaging maker (looks like a trading middleman).
So we should **not** send volume to these on price alone. They need a real factory audit first, and FDC should give us their **exact legal names / GST numbers** so we can check properly.

**3. The alternatives that DO hold up:** **Shrinath Roto Pack** and **Huhtamaki** (laminates, both pharma-grade) and **Berry Global** (bottles, backed by a global giant) are genuinely solid places to move volume — with small caveats noted below.

# Dextrose (your ORS ingredient)

## Gujarat Ambuja Exports — India's biggest maize processor *(you overpay ~₹21 Cr/yr)*
**In one line:** a financially fortress-like, market-leading supplier — the overpayment here is pure margin given away, so negotiate, don't switch.
- **Capacity — huge and growing:** India's largest maize processor at ~5,000 tonnes/day, adding ~1,000 tonnes/day and spending ~₹600 cr on new capacity. Dextrose sits inside their derivatives line, and they're building a dedicated **food-and-pharma-grade** starch unit. They can easily supply FDC.
- **Money — fortress:** credit agencies rate them **AA– (very safe)**; they're **effectively debt-free** (they hold more cash than debt), sales ~₹4,616 cr, ~₹950 cr cash. About as safe as a supplier gets.
- **Warning signs — none material:** only a tiny ₹48 lakh GST demand (being appealed) and a ₹9 lakh insolvency plea that was **dismissed**. (Don't confuse with "Ambuja Cements" — different company.)
- **Pharma fit — yes:** already serves pharma; building a dedicated pharma-grade line.
- **Bottom line:** push the price down — you're overpaying a strong, safe, market leader. No reason to reduce reliance. **Confidence: High.** _[CARE Ratings, 12 Sep 2025]_

## Sukhjit Starch & Chemicals — healthy dextrose maker *(you overpay AND pay too soon)*
**In one line:** a safe, well-run supplier where you hold **two** levers — price and payment timing.
- **Capacity:** 1,600 tonnes/day, guiding toward 2,000; a ₹500 cr new plant planned. Makes real dextrose (monohydrate + anhydrous).
- **Money — healthy:** rated **A (safe)**, low debt (owe ~₹0.55 per ₹1 of own money), ~₹100 cr cash, blue-chip clients (Dabur, Nestlé, Mars, Marico). Note: their profit dipped this year and they run their bank limits ~93% full — so **the early payment you give them is cash they value and you can take back.**
- **Warning signs — none event-driven;** just margin pressure from maize cost.
- **Pharma fit — yes.**
- **Bottom line:** cut the price **and** stop paying early (move to 30-day terms) — they're safe to keep relying on, and both are concessions you can reclaim. **Confidence: High.** _[CRISIL, 12 Jun 2026]_

## Sayaji Industries — cheaper, but financially fragile *(the suggested move-to for dextrose)*
**In one line:** genuinely cheaper and makes real dextrose, but too financially shaky to lean on heavily right now.
- **Capacity:** 950 tonnes/day with real dextrose, **but no meaningful dextrose expansion planned** — only ₹34 cr of automation. So limited room for big extra volume.
- **Money — weak:** their credit grade has slipped three years running to **BB+ (below the "safe" line)**; they made a **loss in FY25** and barely broke even in FY26; owe ~₹2.37 per ₹1 of own money; cash was just ₹4.5 cr and promoters had to put money in to meet debts. "Stretched."
- **Warning signs:** the fragility itself is the risk.
- **Pharma fit — product fits** (80-year maize processor serving pharma/food), though specific pharma certs weren't found.
- **Bottom line:** fine as a **small** second source to keep price honest, but don't move large or sole volume here until their finances recover. **Confidence: High.** _[CARE Ratings, 23 Jun 2026]_

## Universal Starch-Chem Allied — small and opaque *(you overpay)*
**In one line:** overpayment should be clawed back on price, but they're too small and secretive to grow reliance on.
- **Capacity:** small (~750 tonnes/day refining); only started making dextrose in 2023.
- **Money — weak and opaque:** rated **BB and "non-cooperating"** — meaning they **stopped giving the rating agency enough information** (a transparency red flag); high debt; small (~₹490 cr), though last year's profit recovered off a low base.
- **Warning signs:** the non-cooperation flag itself; their plant is in the high-pollution regulatory category (a filing, not a violation).
- **Pharma fit — markets dextrose for pharma**, but no certs/clients found.
- **Bottom line:** push price, don't increase reliance. **Confidence: Medium** (their own opacity limits what anyone can verify). _[Infomerics, 12 May 2025]_

# Laminates / films (your ORS sachet packaging)

## GEEP Industries — a battery brand that also makes film *(you overpay — your biggest laminate line, ₹28 Cr/yr)*
**In one line:** healthy enough to keep buying from, but laminate is a side-business for them, so push price and don't deepen reliance.
- **Who they really are:** "GEEP" is mainly a **batteries / torches / LED brand** (ex-Geep Batteries). Laminate film is just one of several product lines — a ₹200–300 cr diversified firm, not a dedicated packaging house.
- **Capacity:** no verifiable expansion news; the only capacity figure (500 tonnes/month) is self-advertised, not independently confirmed.
- **Money:** unrated, mid-size, and profitability is **softening** (profit down, earnings down). Clean legal record.
- **Pharma fit:** the group says it serves pharma/food, but **no independent pharma certification** was found for GEEP specifically.
- **Bottom line:** push the price (you're overpaying), but **don't grow reliance** — if they ever reprioritise toward batteries, your sachet supply could wobble. **Confidence: Medium.**

## Shrinath Roto Pack — a ~₹1,000-crore Hyderabad packaging maker *(the suggested move-to for laminates)*
**In one line:** quality isn't the question, their debt is the only thing to watch — so use them as a second source, not your only one.
- **Capacity — yes:** two Hyderabad factories making 57,540 tonnes/year (up from ~42,000 in 2022). FDC's ₹6.5 cr is tiny to them. One caution: they expand only when machines hit ~80% full, so **confirm they have spare capacity now** before a big shift.
- **Money — broadly healthy, but carrying real debt:** sales ~₹1,019 cr (the ~8% dip just reflects cheaper plastic passed through, not lost business); profit actually improved. The watch-out: the group borrowed ~₹320 cr for a new aluminium-foil factory, so they owe ~₹2 per ₹1 of own money.
- **Warning signs:** that foil factory runs at only 40–50% and dragged a **one-notch rating cut (to BBB+, Jan 2024)**; one agency (CRISIL) was marked **"non-cooperating" (Aug 2024)**. No court/GST/fraud cases found.
- **Pharma fit — yes:** supplies **GSK**, Britannia, P&G; BRC/ISO certified.
- **Bottom line:** bring them in as a **second laminate source alongside GEEP** — gives you a cheaper price to push GEEP down and a backup — but **dual-source, don't hand them everything** until their foil-plant debt is clearly under control. **Confidence: High.** _[CARE Ratings, 9 Oct 2024; India Ratings; CRISIL]_

## Tetra Pak India — safe global supplier you pay too early *(you pay too soon, ~₹5.5 Cr/yr)*
**In one line:** a rock-solid multinational — there's no reason to be paying them early, and they even finance their own customers.
- **Who/capacity:** their Chakan (Pune) plant is **Tetra Pak's largest outside Sweden**; part of the Tetra Laval group (record ~80bn Swedish kronor revenue).
- **Money:** financially solid MNC, no distress signals.
- **The leverage (key):** Tetra Pak **offers its own customers credit plans and leasing** — so a company that lends to its customers has no basis to make **you** pay almost upfront.
- **Bottom line:** stop paying early; push for 30-day terms, pointing out that they themselves extend credit. **Confidence: Medium** (India-entity accounts aren't public). _[company + trade press, 2025–26]_

## GLS Films — big capacity, heavy debt *(you overpay)*
**In one line:** plenty of capacity and great clients, but financially stretched — so squeeze price, don't deepen reliance.
- **Capacity — strong:** 78,800 tonnes/year at only ~50% use (lots of headroom), blue-chip clients (ITC, PepsiCo, Nestlé, Haldiram).
- **Money — stretched:** **heavily indebted** (owe ~₹5.6 per ₹1 of own money); their earnings barely cover interest (below 1×, propped up by promoter loans). One agency flagged **"non-cooperation"**; another noted **reported FY24 debt-repayment delays** (a second agency then said conduct was in order). Also a past insolvency petition (dismissed by the Supreme Court).
- **Pharma fit — yes** (serves pharma/food/FMCG).
- **Bottom line:** negotiate price hard; **don't increase dependence** without fresh financial comfort. **Confidence: High.** _[CARE; Infomerics, Jun 2025]_

## Huhtamaki India — the strong laminate alternative *(you overpay — small spend today)*
**In one line:** financially very strong and pharma-grade — a genuinely safe place to move laminate volume **and** a price lever.
- **Capacity:** 10+ factories; makes flexible + tube laminates for **pharma**, FMCG; recyclable "blueloop" line.
- **Money — very strong:** rated **AA– (very safe)**, almost no debt (owe ~₹0.17 per ₹1 of own money), >₹400 cr cash, comfortable profits. Revenue flat-ish, which gives **you negotiating room.**
- **Warning signs:** both the MD and CFO resigned in 2025–26 — worth monitoring for continuity, not a red flag yet.
- **Pharma fit — strong** (pharma tube laminates; owns a pharma-label maker).
- **Bottom line:** safe to move volume here and use their flat revenue to push price. Watch the management changes. **Confidence: High.** _[CRISIL, 12 Aug 2026]_

## UFlex — capable but indebted, with a tax-raid history *(you overpay — small spend today)*
**In one line:** India's biggest flexible-packaging firm and a good price benchmark, but don't concentrate volume on them.
- **Capacity — huge:** >1.4 million tonnes/year across many countries, actively expanding.
- **Money — leveraged:** growing (~₹15,513 cr revenue), but a credit agency just moved their **outlook to "Negative"** because of **heavy debt-funded expansion** (debt ~4.25× earnings).
- **Warning signs — material:** **income-tax department raided ~84 of their premises in 2023** over alleged tax evasion; the stock fell ~25%; related tax litigation is **still unresolved**. (No SEBI/ED action found.)
- **Pharma fit — yes** (full laminate capability).
- **Bottom line:** use them as a second source to benchmark/push price, but **don't shift major volume** given the debt and unresolved tax overhang. **Confidence: High.** _[CRISIL, 6 Aug 2026; press, 2023–25]_

## Silk Polypack — we can't find them *(the suggested move-to)*
**In one line:** an exhaustive search found **no company matching this name** — so we can't recommend moving anything here.
- We searched every company registry, trade directory and rating agency, plus spelling variants; their supposed website doesn't even resolve. The same registries readily returned data on the other suppliers, so this is a **genuine blank, not a search failure.**
- **Bottom line:** **do not move volume here.** Ask FDC for the exact legal name, GST number and CIN off a real invoice, then we re-check. **Confidence: High that nothing is verifiable today.**

# Rigid plastic (your bottles, caps, jars)

## Chemco Plastic Industries — strong, pharma-grade bottle maker *(flagged for old stock, ~₹5.5 Cr/yr)*
**In one line:** a strong, well-rated supplier — the "old stock" is how they run their business, not a sign of trouble; fix it with a contract clause.
- **Capacity:** processes >125,000 tonnes/year across 23 locations; building a new recycled-PET unit.
- **Money — strong:** rated **A+ (safe)**, revenue grew ₹602 cr → ₹939 cr in three years, good margins, planning a reported ₹1,500 cr IPO.
- **Why the old stock:** they deliberately **hold ~125 days of inventory** as a supply hedge (a structural choice), not a distress sign.
- **Pharma fit — yes:** clients include **Cipla, Johnson & Johnson**, Coca-Cola, Nestlé.
- **Bottom line:** keep them — but since old stock matters for ORS shelf life, add a **"freshest-first / maximum-age-at-dispatch" clause** to the contract. **Confidence: High.** _[CRISIL, 1 Sep 2025]_

## TPAC Packaging India — strong, well-rated bottle maker *(you overpay — small spend)*
**In one line:** a healthy, A-rated, pharma-capable supplier — the overpayment is a negotiation, not a risk.
- **Capacity:** multiple plants (west/north/south India), bottles + caps, backed by Thailand's Thai Plaspac.
- **Money — strong:** rated **A (safe)**, revenue ₹822 cr, low debt, 15% growth. (Profit dipped last year on margin pressure.)
- **Pharma fit — yes** (serves pharma + food; marquee clients Amul, Parle).
- **Bottom line:** push price, ideally with a **resin-cost-linked clause** (their costs swing with plastic prices). Safe to keep/grow. **Confidence: High.** _[ICRA, 27 Mar 2026]_

## Berry Global India — backed by a global giant *(you overpay + a suggested move-to)*
**In one line:** the most financially secure of the bottle options — safe to move to, **if** they actually make your specific bottle.

- **Capacity:** expanded Bangalore healthcare-packaging site — **but it leans toward drug-delivery devices**, so we must confirm they make FDC's plain HDPE/PET ORS bottles, not just devices.
- **Pharma fit — excellent** (that's why they're a premium/overpay line).
- **Bottom line:** safe to move volume and use the premium as leverage — **first confirm the India plant makes your exact bottle SKUs.** **Confidence: High on the parent, Medium on local product-fit.** _[S&P, May 2025]_
